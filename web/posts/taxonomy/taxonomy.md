## Introduction

Plants are classified into various categories based on their characteristics and evolutionary relationships.
Understanding the taxonomy of a plant species involves familiarizing oneself with terms like *family*, *genus*,
*species*,
*subspecies*, *variety*, and *cultivar*.

Let's explain what the different terms mean.

### Family

A **family** in plant taxonomy represents a higher-level grouping that includes multiple genera sharing certain common
traits. For instance, the <span id="familia-taxon">Araceae</span> family encompasses various genera such
as <span id="genus-taxon">Anthurium</span>, <span id="genus-taxon">Monstera</span> and <span id="genus-taxon">
Philodendron</span>.

### Genus

A **genus** is a level below the family and groups together species that are closely related.

### Species

A **species** is the basic unit of classification. It refers to a group of organisms that share similar characteristics
and can interbreed to produce fertile offspring. Scientific names of species are composed of two words: the genus name (
italicized) followed by the species name (not italicized).

### Subspecies

Subspecies, positioned between a species and a variety, denotes a geographically isolated plant within a species. This
isolation prevents interbreeding despite genetic compatibility. This geographical distinction often results in varied
physical traits.

The notation "subsp." appears between the species and subspecies names.

### Variety

A **variety** denotes a naturally occurring variation within a species. It is indicated by abbreviating "var." before
the variety name, which is not italicized. For instance, *Rosa indica var. odorata* represents a variety of the garden
rose with a distinct scent.

### Cultivar

A **cultivar** is a cultivated variety developed through selective breeding or other human interventions. It can be seen
as a variety produced by human selection and breeding rather than naturally occurring.

Cultivar names are capitalized and enclosed in single quotes but are not italicized. For example, 'Peace' is a cultivar
of the garden rose.

Understanding these taxonomic terms helps scientists, horticulturists, and enthusiasts communicate precisely about plant
species, their relationships, and their variations.

## Common names

Each plant species has a scientific name. Often, the plant will also have common names. These are names that people use
to refer to the plant. For example, the species <span id="species-taxon">Pilea peperomioides</span> is also called the
*pass-along plant*. This common name refers to the fact that this plant is very easily propagated and passed along as a
gift.

Not only species, but also genera and familiae may have common names. For example,
the <span id="familia-taxon">Araceae</span> family is often referred to as the **arum family** or the **aroids**.
